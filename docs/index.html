Simplicity at your fingertips.

Everything you need is just a click away in your menubar. No more hunting through Finder to eject your drives safely.

Designed for macOS.

MountMate feels right at home on your Mac. With full support for Dark Mode and native design paradigms, it's the drive manager macOS should have included.

More than a mount button.

Network shares, automatic reconnects and shortcuts for the drives you keep plugged in all day.

Network shares

Mount SMB and NFS shares from the menu bar (existing AFP shares keep working with a deprecation notice) and auto-mount them at login, or all at once with Mount All and Unmount All in the Saved Network Shares header. Mounted shares show how much space is used and free. Moving to a new Mac? Export your saved shares to a file and import them there (passwords stay behind).

Keep Alive

Mark a share or volume as Keep Mounted and MountMate reconnects it when it drops, after a network change, on wake and at login.

Mount Read-Only

Right-click an unmounted volume to mount it read-only, and the row shows a Read-only badge.

Unmount When Idle

Unmount a volume on an external drive after 1 to 60 minutes without reads or writes. Never forced, so a volume in use stays mounted.

Eject all external disks

One click unmounts and ejects every external disk so you can unplug them before leaving. Internal disks and disks with a protected volume stay, nothing is forced, and one message names any disk that would not eject.

Keyboard shortcuts

Unmount All with ⌘⌥⇧U and Mount All with ⌘⇧M by default; Eject All External Disks and Mount or Unmount All Network Shares have none until you record one. Change them in Settings; the keys shown follow your keyboard layout. A small message at the top of the screen says what the shortcut did.

Shortcuts

Mount All Volumes, Unmount All Volumes, Eject All External Disks, Mount and Unmount All Network Shares, Mount and Unmount Network Share, Mount and Unmount Volume, and Eject Volume (pick a volume, even two with the same name) are actions in the Shortcuts app, so “arrive home” can mount your NAS and “before bed” can unmount your Time Machine disk. Failures show up as the shortcut’s error.

Block at Startup

Stop selected disks from mounting when plugged in or at login. Optionally add a rule to /etc/fstab so macOS leaves them alone even before MountMate opens.

Encrypted volumes

Unlock encrypted APFS volumes from the menu bar and optionally remember the password in your Keychain; it is saved only after the volume unlocks, and you can forget it at any time. Keep Alive, Mount Again on Wake and Mount All use a saved password without asking.

Copy Path / Open in Terminal

Right-click a mounted volume or network share to copy its mount path or open a Terminal window in that folder.

Rename Volume

Right-click a mounted, writable external volume and choose Rename Volume… to give it a new name. The name is checked as you type, and an alert explains it if macOS cannot rename the volume.

Verify Volume

Right-click a mounted external volume and choose Verify Volume to check its file system in the background. It never repairs anything; if problems are found, it points you to First Aid in Disk Utility.

Browse Network

The network share sheet can list the SMB and NFS servers announcing themselves on your local network. Pick one to fill in the address and protocol, or type it by hand as before.

Open in Finder after mounting

Optional, off by default (Settings → General → Behavior). A volume or share you mount yourself opens in a Finder window once it is ready. Automatic mounts never open a window.

Force Unmount for shares

When a network share is busy, its server went away or the unmount timed out, MountMate offers a confirmed Force Unmount instead of a raw error message.

Reconnect Now

While Keep Alive waits to reconnect a share or volume, its row shows "Retrying in N seconds" and a Reconnect Now button that tries again right away. The menu bar icon gets a Wi-Fi badge until everything is back.

Update reminders

An update found by the automatic check never opens a window over your work. The menu bar icon gets a small dot and the menu shows "MountMate X.Y is available" with a View… button.

Force Eject

When a drive is busy, MountMate names the app using it and offers to force the eject. It asks first, so nothing is forced by accident.

Full Disk Access

Optional. Turn it on for MountMate in System Settings → Privacy & Security if some disks or their storage details are missing from the list. Settings → General shows whether it is granted.

Unmount on Sleep, Mount Again on Wake

Optional. Unmount every removable disk before the Mac sleeps, so closing the lid never pulls a drive out from under a file. Mount Again on Wake brings those disks back after wake; disks you unmounted yourself stay unmounted.

Unmount When the Screen Locks

Optional. Unmount every removable disk the moment the screen locks, so a drive with private files is never left open while you are away. Protected and Keep Alive volumes stay mounted; Mount Again on Wake brings the rest back when you unlock.

Ignore, Protect, Pin

Right-click a volume to hide it, keep it out of Unmount All and the sleep unmount, or pin it to the top of the list. Review the lists in Settings → Management.

Almost-full warning

A volume that is nearly full (90% by default, your choice of 80, 90 or 95%) gets an orange badge, and with Notifications on one notice per mount, before copies and backups start failing. External drives that stay plugged in are checked every 15 minutes. Nothing is unmounted or deleted.

SMART warning

A drive that reports a failing SMART health status gets a red warning next to its name, so you can back it up before it dies. Nothing is unmounted for you.

Notifications

Optional, off by default. Get told once when a mount cannot be reconnected and when it is back, instead of on every retry. The notice has a Reconnect Now button, and clicking it opens MountMate. You can also be told when a USB drive or memory card is pulled out without ejecting, so you can check your files.

Questions, answered.

The ones we hear most often.

Can I install MountMate with Homebrew?

Yes. Run brew install mountmate in Terminal. The app needs macOS 13 Ventura or later and updates itself afterwards (Settings → About & Updates), or with brew upgrade mountmate.

Why does MountMate ask for my administrator password?

Only to change /etc/fstab, which is needed for "Also Block at Startup" and for custom mount points of volumes. Mounting, unmounting and everything else never ask. Since macOS 27 the standard system prompt can no longer write /etc/fstab, so MountMate shows its own password dialog and hands the password to sudo; it is never stored or logged. If macOS still refuses (“Operation not permitted”), MountMate says so and you can add the rule yourself in Terminal with sudo vifs. This needs version 6.2.1 or later.

A share fails with “access denied”. What can I check?

The server accepted the connection but refused this account for the share (EACCES). Check the account's permissions on the server. For a Windows domain account, enter the username as DOMAIN\user or user@domain. If it started after a server update, ask the administrator whether the server still accepts SMB 2 or 3 with NTLMv2 sign-in. The alert ends with the original error code (for example Error code: 80 (EAUTH)); please include it when you open an issue.

Where are my passwords stored?

In the macOS Keychain, never in a file and never in the logs. For an encrypted APFS volume the password is saved only if you choose to remember it, and only after it has unlocked the volume; right-click the volume and pick “Forget Saved Password” to remove it. Items are labeled “MountMate – name”, so they are easy to find in Keychain Access.

How does Keep Alive decide when to reconnect?

For every share or volume marked “Keep Mounted”, MountMate retries at the interval you choose in Settings (10 seconds to 5 minutes), and also right after a network change, after the Mac wakes and at login. With Notifications on you get one message when a mount stays down and one when it is back. Checking a share only reads the Mac's own mount table, so a NAS that is not in use can still go to sleep.

Why was my volume not unmounted when idle?

Unmount When Idle watches reads and writes on the whole disk, so any other volume or Spotlight activity on the same disk counts as use. The unmount is never forced: if the volume is busy it stays mounted and is tried again after another idle period. It only applies to volumes on external drives, never to internal disks, disk images, network shares or volumes marked Keep Mounted or Protected.

Can I change the keyboard shortcuts?

Yes. Open Settings, click a shortcut and press the new keys. It must include ⌘ or ⌃, and some combinations are reserved by macOS or another app. The defaults are ⌘⌥⇧U to unmount all disks and ⌘⇧M to mount them.

How do I send logs with a bug report?

Open Settings → General → Logs and click Copy Diagnostics, then paste the result into your report. It holds the MountMate and macOS versions, your on/off settings and the last hour of MountMate's log. Passwords, paths, and disk and share names are left out.
